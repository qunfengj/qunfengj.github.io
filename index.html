Could a planet’s intense heating be much younger than the planet itself?

I use joint radial-velocity and transit-timing posteriors with secular tidal models to constrain how long L 98-59 b’s inferred present-day tidal heating can be sustained—and to reconstruct its tidal dissipation history.

Connected questions.

When a giant moves inward,
what survives?

With Juliette Becker, I combine REBOUND/REBOUNDx simulations and tidal calculations to test whether lower-mass giants can migrate inward on highly eccentric orbits while preserving smaller inner planets.

Our calculations explore a promising window for lower-mass giants: gentler perturbations and shorter circularization times under the adopted tidal prescription.

More comets inward.
Less material intact?

Getting a comet to an inner planet is only part of the story. The impact speed helps determine whether fragile prebiotic molecules survive delivery.

Across 64,000 simulated comet trajectories, modest planetary eccentricity enhances inward transport while suppressing the gentlest impacts.

Read the study

Why are some
worlds missing?

Neptune-sized planets are scarce on very short orbits. Using 1,850 Kepler and TESS planets, stellar activity models, and null tests, I test whether lifetime XUV exposure explains how this desert differs between stellar types.

Cumulative XUV makes the boundaries more alike. A statistical null test shows that this alignment alone does not establish a universal erosion threshold.

Read the paper

A giant that excites—and constrains.

Can the outer giant explain candidate planet d’s eccentric orbit? I combine 772 published radial velocities with three-dimensional integrations to test both excitation and survival.

Explore π Mensae

Quiet orbits around two suns.

Were these planets born dynamically cold, or did stability select the survivors? With Man Hoi Lee, I use free eccentricities and population comparisons to test a stability-filter hypothesis.

Can sunlight reverse
orbital migration?

With Wen-Han Zhou, I combine three-dimensional eclipse geometry and heat-conduction models to study how orbital inclination changes the rate and direction of thermal migration in binary asteroids.

What shapes
planetary eccentricities?

With Gregory J. Gilbert, I use hierarchical Bayesian models to study how eccentricity varies with orbital period and observed multiplicity in super-Earth and sub-Neptune populations.

Other questions I am exploring

How did Pluto’s small moons get here?

Free-eccentricity measurements and tidal-evolution tests constrain early in-situ formation in the Pluto–Charon system, with Man Hoi Lee.

Before invoking an unseen planet

Using TESS eclipse timing and stellar models, I reassess apsidal-motion planet claims in CH Ind and SW CMa. Accounting for stellar tidal systematics leaves no significant third-body signal.

Let the present
constrain the past.

I connect individual planetary systems with population-level patterns. Numerical dynamics, observations, and statistical inference help test which histories can explain the worlds we see—and where an appealing explanation remains uncertain.
